START / REVIEW & SAVE

Capture

Describe an action or context, review the interpretation, then save the records together.

What you can test
  • Interpret a sentence into a task, reminder, or note. Nothing is saved before confirmation.
  • Include a note with an action to link it automatically. Ambiguous times require clarification.
  • Use “before” to reference an existing task without inventing a reminder time. Choose a Context task for “before that.”
  • Changing a field requires another preview.
  • Set a deadline separately from the reminder time; choose daily or weekly repeats.
  • If confirmation is interrupted, retry the same proposal with the same key, even after reloading this tab.

Conversation test

Start a sentence, answer questions, then say “yes” to save. Sessions survive reloads. Try “I have an interview at Ather on Tuesday”, then answer “3pm” when asked for the time. “Action” also means task. Try “the Ather one”, “tomorrow at 9am”, “one day before”, “no reminder”, or “cancel”. Use “replace:” to rewrite the request.

Single sentence and explicit capture tools

English phrases: tomorrow, end of day (11:59 PM), weekdays, in 10 minutes, daily/weekly, and explicit dates. Include am/pm. Use “by” for a deadline, “note:” for context, and “before my interview at Ather” for a dependency. Reminder requests suggest related tasks. Dated context supplies a proposed reminder time using the lead time above; undated context still needs timing. You can also say “one day before my interview.” Interpretation runs locally.
Supported time phrases and defaults
  • End of day / EOD: today at 11:59 PM. End of tomorrow: tomorrow at 11:59 PM.
  • End of week / EOW: this Sunday at 11:59 PM. End of month / EOM: last calendar day at 11:59 PM.
  • Close of business / COB / end of work day: 5 PM (no holiday calendar).
  • Morning: 9 AM. Afternoon: 3 PM. Evening: 6 PM. Night / tonight: 8 PM. Prefix with “this” or “tomorrow”; defaults are shown before saving.
  • In half an hour: 30 minutes. A quarter hour: 15 minutes. A couple of hours: two hours. “After two hours” also works.
  • Explicit clocks override period defaults. “By” sets a task deadline; a “remind me” sentence creates reminder timing.
